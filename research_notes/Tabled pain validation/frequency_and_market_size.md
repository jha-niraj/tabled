# How often the "unfamiliar menu at the table" moment happens, and how big the addressable market is (India first, global second) - as of October 2026

Scope note: researched 2026-10-09. Web search budget ran out part-way through; later steps used direct page fetches only. Several primary PDFs (NRAI IFSR 2024, Bain "How India Eats", Swiggy-Kearney 2025) were not directly readable, so their figures are taken from secondary coverage that quotes them, and that is flagged on each line. Every number carries the year it refers to.

Headline, brutally honest: the pure dine-out (not delivery) frequency of a metro Indian is about 3 to 3.6 visits a month (NRAI city data, 2023-24), not the 7.9 "dining out" figure that is usually quoted (that one includes ordering in). Non-native cuisines (Korean, Japanese, Vietnamese, Mexican) are growing off a tiny base - 528 Japanese restaurants in the whole country - and biryani and North Indian still dominate. The moment is nonetheless not rare: with 70 to 100 million eating-out consumers in the top 8 metros, 3+ visits a month, parties of about 3, and a third of table-service occasions at venues where at least one person meets a dish they do not recognise, the base-case estimate is on the order of 130 million table-occasions a year in the top 8 metros (range 45 to 300 million). The honest weakness is that no survey directly measures "did not recognise a dish"; that part of the estimate is assumption, labelled below.

## Key question 1: How often do urban Indians eat out, by city tier and income?

### Takeaway
NRAI IFSR 2024 reports 7.9 eating-out occasions per month (2023-24), but its own city chapters show that figure combines dine-out and order-in; pure dine-out in metros is about 3.1 (Hyderabad) to 3.6 (Chennai) times a month. Bain-Swiggy (2024) puts India at about 5 times a month versus 25 to 30 in the US and China. No tier-by-income frequency table was found.

### Cited Findings
- "The average Indian now dines out 7.9 times per month, a 20% increase from 6.6 times in 2018-19" (NRAI IFSR 2024, quoted in a June 2025 industry report that reproduces NRAI charts) — [Tea Post industry report citing NRAI IFSR 2024](https://teapost.in/doc-files/tea-post-limited-industry-report.pdf); same 7.9 figure in — [Business Standard, Jul 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/indian-food-services-sector-to-grow-by-8-1-from-2024-to-2028-report-124070900997_1.html)
- Hyderabad (NRAI IFSR 2024): "Average dine-out frequency 6.96 times per month. Residents dine out 3.09 times per month and order in 3.87 times." Average spend per dine-out visit Rs 990 — [Deccan Chronicle on NRAI IFSR 2024 Hyderabad chapter](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/telangana/hyderabads-food-services-industry-booms-nrais-2024-report-1809982)
- Chennai (NRAI IFSR 2024): residents dine out 3.6 times a month, order in 4.28 times a month, spend Rs 825 per person; Chennai market Rs 15.6K crore, 4th largest city — [NRAI Chennai chapter launch, Hospitality Lexis](https://hospitalitylexis.media/national-restaurant-association-of-india-nrai-launches-india-food-services-report-2024-chennai-chapter/); a competing headline says "dine out 4.28 times monthly", which appears to swap the dine-out and order-in numbers — [Hospitality Biz India](https://hospitalitybizindia.com/news-track/chennai-ranks-4th-in-indias-food-services-market-at-inr-15-6k-crores-residents-dine-out-4-28-times-monthly-spend-inr-825-per-person-says-report/)
- Bain-Swiggy "How India Eats" (Jul 2024): Indians eat out about 5 times a month, expected to reach 7 to 8 by 2030; the US and China are at 25 to 30 times a month; 70% of food services consumption in 2023 came from the top 50 cities and upper-middle and high-income segments; the addressable eating-out consumer base is 320 to 340 million today, expected to be 430 to 450 million by 2030; each online customer orders from 6+ restaurants and 3+ cuisines a year — [Startup Story Media summarising Bain-Swiggy](https://startupstorymedia.com/insights-online-food-delivery-market-set-to-reach-rs-2-trillion-by-2030-bain-swiggy-report/); the "5 a month, special event" framing also in — [Business Standard, Jul 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/food-delivery-market-to-grow-18-to-rs-2-trn-by-2030-bain-swiggy-report-124070300980_1.html)
- Comparator monthly frequencies attributed to the same Bain-Swiggy study: China 33, US 27, Singapore 19, South Korea 14, Vietnam 11, India 5 — [Indus Food article (search snippet; the page itself did not render these figures on fetch)](https://indusfood.co.in/article/indian-food-services/)
- Rakuten Insight APAC dining survey (May 2025, base 92,140 across 12 markets), India column of "How often do you dine out/buy meals outside the home?": 15% at least once every day, 23% several times a week, 23% once a week, 16% several times a month, 16% only on special occasions, 7% never. Where Indians usually go (multi-select): casual dining 52%, street food 50%, fast-food chains 50%, food courts 47%, fine dining 45%, cafes 43%. 45% of Indians usually make a reservation (highest with China). Indians usually dine with family (72%) and friends (65%); only 12% alone — [Rakuten Insight, Dining out habits in APAC, May 2025 (PDF)](https://insight.rakuten.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/RI-Dining-Out-2025.pdf)
- Tier-II and Tier-III cities' contribution to India's food service market grew from 21% in 2016 to 32% in 2023 (NRAI, quoted) — [Tea Post industry report citing NRAI](https://teapost.in/doc-files/tea-post-limited-industry-report.pdf)
- Swiggy-Kearney "How India Eats" (2025): dining-out growth outside the top 8 cities is 2x that of the top 8; pre-booked tables growing 7x faster than walk-ins; Gen Z growing 3x faster than other cohorts — [Entrepreneur India on Swiggy-Kearney 2025](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/swiggy-kearney-report-projects-125-billion-food-market/500233)
- Deloitte-FICCI (May 2025): nearly 50% of urban consumers' food budgets go to prepared foods, dining out and deliveries — [WION on Deloitte-FICCI](https://www.wionews.com/business-economy/urban-indians-now-spend-half-their-food-budget-outside-the-home-highlights-deloitte-ficci-report-1747910992609/amp)
- NRAI IFSR 2024 consumer research covered 5,300+ people across 40+ cities; operator survey 160+ operators across 50+ cities — [NRAI newsletter Apr-Jun 2024](https://conference.nrai.org/files/uploads/2024/08/april-june-2024-newsletter.pdf)
- Zomato Gold (2019-era, Redseer-validated): dining frequency of Gold members rose from 2.8 to 3.3 times/month after joining; 90% of members tried new restaurants because of the programme — [Zomato blog H1 FY2020 report (surfaced in search, not fetched)](https://blog.zomato.com/h1-fy2020-report)

### Inferences
- The widely quoted 7.9/month is eat-out plus order-in. For a QR-at-the-table product only the dine-out half matters: about 3.1 to 3.6 visits a month in metros, i.e. 37 to 43 sit-down occasions a year per metro consumer. The Zomato Gold 2.8 to 3.3 figure (2019) is consistent with that.
- Rakuten's India sample (online panel, skews urban and affluent) says 61% eat out at least weekly; the 23% "only on special occasions or never" tail is the senior's "occasional" cohort, and it is a minority of urban panel respondents.
- 70% of consumption concentrated in the top 50 cities and upper-middle/high income means the addressable "moments" are also concentrated where Tabled would sell first.

### Gaps
- No frequency table by city tier x income was found; NRAI's full PDF is not public and Statista's Nov 2024 India dining survey (20,914 respondents) is paywalled.
- The 7.9 national figure could not be verified against the NRAI PDF directly; the city chapters are the evidence that it blends dine-out and delivery.

## Key question 2: Number of organised dine-in restaurants, and dine-in share by cuisine

### Takeaway
India's food services market was Rs 5.69 lakh crore in FY24 with the organised segment at 43.86% (Rs 2.5 trillion); casual dining is 48% of organised and QSR 27%. The only hard outlet count found is Hyderabad: 74,807 restaurants, 41,144 organised, 16,379 of them cloud kitchens. No source gives a dine-in share by cuisine (North Indian vs Chinese vs Pan-Asian etc.); that is a real gap.

This is an extrapolation from one city, not a sourced count.
- Delivery data (biryani, pizza, North Indian) over-represents familiar comfort food; dine-in cuisine mix is likely more diverse (Rakuten: 45% of Indian urban respondents "usually" go to fine dining), but no dine-in cuisine split exists to prove it.

### Gaps
- No national count of organised dine-in restaurants; no dine-in share by cuisine (North Indian / South Indian / Chinese / Pan-Asian / Thai / Japanese / Korean / Italian / Continental / Mediterranean). Zomato and Swiggy publish delivery rankings only.

## Key question 3: Growth of Pan-Asian, Japanese, Korean, Mediterranean restaurants in Indian metros 2022-2026

### Takeaway
Korean is the fastest-growing cuisine on Indian platforms (17x in orders 2020-2024, Zomato data cited by Swiggy-Kearney and Mordor), with Vietnamese 6x and Mexican ~4x, but these are multiples off a tiny base: India had only 528 Japanese restaurants in October 2025 (+5% vs 2023). Hyper-regional Indian cuisines are growing 2 to 8x faster than mainstream formats, which also creates unfamiliar menus for non-locals. No city-level Pan-Asian or Mediterranean outlet growth data was found.

### Cited Findings
- Swiggy-Kearney "How India Eats" (2025): Korean food up 17x, Vietnamese 6x, Mexican nearly 4x; hyper-regional Indian categories growing 2 to 8x faster than mainstream formats; search interest up 11x for boba tea and 4x for matcha (comparison periods not stated in coverage) — [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/swiggy-kearney-report-projects-125-billion-food-market/500233)
- The same multiples with period 2020 to 2024, attributed to "aggregator data compiled by Zomato": Korean orders 17-fold, Vietnamese 6-fold, Mexican 3.7-fold — [Mordor Intelligence, India QSR market](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/india-quick-service-restaurant-market) (note: the two sources are not independent; both appear to rest on platform order data)
- India has 528 Japanese restaurants as of October 2025, up 5.02% from 2023; Maharashtra, Delhi and Haryana lead; Japanese food market in India USD 277 million in 2024 (Data Bridge Market Research); 233,000 Indian visitors to Japan in 2024 (JNTO). The article is a franchise-promotion piece and does not name the source of the 528 count — [Indian Retailer / FranchiseTV](https://www.indianretailer.com/franchisetv/archives/article/novelty-network-how-sushi-restaurant-brands-are-scaling-through-franchising)
- Godrej Food Trends Report 2024 (190+ experts): mainstreaming of Korean cuisine named a key 2024 shift, "Korean food transitioned from a trend to a regular menu item"; Knorr Korean ramen and McDonald's India Korean-inspired menu cited; no outlet counts — [Restaurant India on Godrej Food Trends 2024](https://www.restaurantindia.in/news/india-s-hot-sauce-surge-and-k-food-boom-led-2024-s-dining-trends.n12799)
- Euromonitor (Jun 2021): 88% of Indian K-drama fans willing to try Korean food, 40% had never tasted it; K-noodle makers advised to target "a niche base of consumers who are experimental and willing to pay a premium" — [Euromonitor, The rise and future of K-food in India](https://www.euromonitor.com/article/the-rise-and-future-of-k-food-in-india-food-provenance-in-consumer-decision-making)
- Fine dining grew ~105% and luxury dining ~120% "recently" (Mordor, period not stated; treat as unverified) — [Restroworks round-up](https://www.restroworks.com/blog/indian-restaurant-industry-statistics/)
- Younger cohorts "are particularly responsive to international culinary movements and are more willing to experiment with foreign cuisines"; Gen Z and millennials account for ~40% of food services consumption (2024) — [Tea Post industry report](https://teapost.in/doc-files/tea-post-limited-industry-report.pdf)

### Inferences
- 17x growth on a base that produced 528 Japanese outlets nationally means non-native East Asian cuisine is still a low-single-digit percent of dine-in occasions in 2026. The honest framing is "fast-growing niche", not "mainstream".
- The bigger unfamiliarity driver in India is probably intra-Indian: hyper-regional menus (Chettinad, Kerala, Northeast, Kashmiri, Goan) growing 2 to 8x faster than mainstream formats, served to a migrant and travelling population that does not know them.

### Gaps
- No Economic Times / Mint / Ken article with city-level Korean or Japanese outlet counts for Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi, Hyderabad or Pune 2022-2026 was retrieved (search budget ran out before this; fetched pieces were promotional lists).
- No Pan-Asian, Thai or Mediterranean restaurant-count growth data for India at all.

## Key question 4: India tourism, internal migration, and language diversity at the table

### Takeaway
India had 91.5 lakh foreign tourist arrivals in 2025 (down 8%) and 428.55 crore domestic tourist visits (up 45%, inflated by the 2025 Kumbh Mela in UP). Only about 10.6% of Indians speak any English (2011 Census) and about 3 to 6% fluently; Hindi is the first language of 43.6%, and 22 scheduled languages each carry a regional cuisine vocabulary, so a large share of diners at a non-local restaurant cannot read the dish names in the menu's language. Migration data was not retrievable (403).

### Cited Findings
- Foreign tourist arrivals 2025: 91.49 lakh, down 8.1% from 99.52 lakh in 2024; international arrivals including Indian nationals living abroad 2.02 crore (down 1.7%); domestic tourist visits to states/UTs 428.55 crore (up 45.5%); foreign tourist visits to states 2.53 crore (up 21.6%); foreign exchange earnings USD 31.69 billion (down 9.5%); Uttar Pradesh 155.74 crore domestic visits (up from 64.68 crore), accounting for ~68% of the national rise, including 2025 Kumbh Mela visits; visits are not unique persons — [Policy Edge, citing Ministry of Tourism India Tourism Data Compendium 2026](https://www.policyedge.in/p/domestic-tourism-surges-in-2025-but-foreign-arrivals-and-earnings-fall)
- CAPA: ~9 million foreign tourist arrivals in calendar 2025, down 9.4% year-on-year (Ministry of Tourism report of 11 Feb 2026) — [CAPA Centre for Aviation](https://centreforaviation.com/news/india-records-9m-foreign-tourist-arrivals-in-2025-down-9-1348185)
- Domestic tourist visits 2024: 2,948.191 million (Ministry compendium 2025, via secondary summary) — [search summary; Ministry snapshot PDF returned 403 on fetch](https://tourism.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-02/Annual%20Tourism%20Snapshot%202025.pdf)
- Census 2011: Hindi first language 43.63%; 22 scheduled languages; 13 languages each over 1% (Bengali 8.03%, Marathi 6.86%, Telugu 6.70%, Tamil 5.70%, Gujarati 4.58%, Urdu 4.19%, Kannada 3.61%, Odia 3.10%, Malayalam 2.88%, Punjabi 2.74%); 26% of Indians bilingual, 7% trilingual; English total 128,539,090 speakers (10.62%), of which first language 259,678 (0.02%), second language 82.7 million, third 45.6 million — [Wikipedia, List of languages by number of native speakers in India (Census 2011)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_languages_by_number_of_native_speakers_in_India)
- India Human Development Survey 2005: fluent English 5% of men and 3% of women; "at least some English" 28% of men, 17% of women — [Wikipedia, Indian English (citing IHDS 2005)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_English)
- Lok Foundation-Oxford survey 2019: 6% of respondents spoke English; 12% urban vs 3% rural — [The News Minute on Lok Foundation survey](https://www.thenewsminute.com/amp/story/news/90-in-hindi-belt-speak-only-one-language-rest-of-india-more-bilingual-report)
- Mastercard (2025, New York): Indian tourists spend 15% of their dining wallet at restaurants serving their native cuisine (Chinese tourists 17%) — [Mastercard, Where do tourists take a bite out of the Big Apple (search snippet)](https://www.mastercard.com/us/en/news-and-trends/stories/2025/where-do-tourists-take-a-bite-out-of-the-big-apple.html)

### Inferences
- With ~89% of Indians speaking no English and only ~3 to 6% fluent, an English-only menu in a metro restaurant is unreadable or only partly readable for a large share of the domestic travelling and migrant population; the moment "I cannot tell what this dish is" is partly a language problem, not only a cuisine-knowledge problem, which is Tabled's any-Indian-language angle.
- Indian tourists abroad spending 85% of their dining wallet outside native cuisine (Mastercard NYC 2025) is indirect evidence that Indians do eat unfamiliar menus when travelling.
- Domestic tourist visits (even discounting Kumbh: 2024's 2.95 billion) dwarf foreign arrivals 300:1; the unfamiliar-regional-menu moment in India is overwhelmingly a domestic traveller and migrant phenomenon.

### Gaps
- Internal migration figures (Census 2011 inter-state stock, Economic Survey 2016-17 annual flow) could not be fetched (Wikipedia page 404, Economic Survey PDF 403). Not included rather than quoted from memory.
- No survey measures what share of diners cannot read the menu's language in Indian restaurants.
- Foreign tourists' cuisine choices inside India: no data.

## Key question 5: Global dining-out frequency and international tourism 2025-2026

### Takeaway
International arrivals hit a record 1.52 billion in 2025 (+4%), receipts USD 1.9 trillion; 2026 outlook +3 to 4%. Dining-out is far more frequent abroad than in India: 42% of Americans and 39% of Singaporeans dine out at least weekly (2024-25), 68% of Singaporeans and 59% of Thais eat out several times a week or daily (2025). Tourists put roughly 20 to 36% of their spend on food, depending on city.

### Cited Findings

- US: 42% of consumers dine out at least once a week (up from 39%), 44% order takeout/delivery weekly; average dine-out spend USD 54; 74% prefer viewing a physical menu and ordering from a server; Harris Poll for TouchBistro, 1,500 US diners, Oct 15-25 2024 — [TouchBistro 2025 American Diner Trends Report](https://marketingdev.touchbistro.com/?p=37255); income split in the same report: 27% of households under USD 50k vs 64% of households USD 200k+ dine out weekly — [BusinessWire release (search snippet)](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250401936078/en/TouchBistro-2025-American-Diner-Trends-Report-Shows-Resilience-in-Consumer-Habits-Signaling-Optimism-for-Restaurants)

### Inferences
- Globally the moment scales with 1.52 billion international trips in 2025 x roughly 2 to 3 restaurant meals a day x a quarter to a third of spend on food; every one of those meals is by definition at an unfamiliar menu, often in an unfamiliar language.
- For a restaurant-pays product, markets with weekly dining (US, Singapore, Thailand, UAE tourist hubs) generate 5 to 10x the occasions per consumer that India does; India's advantage is volume of people and language diversity, not frequency.

### Gaps
- UAE dine-in frequency: only a ~2022 YouGov takeaway figure (23% order takeaway several times a week) surfaced; no dine-in number.
- Japan daily/weekly frequency beyond Rakuten's column; Statista's Apr 2025 Japan survey is paywalled.

## Key question 6: Evidence that tourists and diners struggle with menus; first-visit and never-had-it shares

### Takeaway
Evidence that menus are a problem for travellers is consistent but mostly qualitative: dining out ranked second of four obstacles abroad (TransPerfect 2010), 35% of US travellers point at menus to avoid pronouncing dish names and 17% retreat to American or fast food to avoid ordering mistakes (Preply, Jun 2025), and 59% of experienced travellers use translation apps on trips (Global Rescue, Fall 2025). No source measures the share of restaurant visits that are first visits or the share of orders that are never-before-eaten dishes; no Google Lens menu-usage statistics exist publicly.

### Cited Findings
- TransPerfect travel survey (Nov 2010, sample size not stated): dining out - ordering food, reading menus, communicating with wait staff - ranked second of the top four troubles tourists face abroad, after public transport; no percentages — [TransPerfect release](https://www.transperfect.com/about/news/survey-reveals-top-four-travel-troubles-tourists-face)
- Preply (US adults): 35% point at menus to avoid pronouncing foreign words; 17% stick to American or fast food to avoid language slip-ups; 26% feel scared or intimidated to speak a foreign language; 32% intentionally choose destinations where English is widely spoken; 45% would travel more if they spoke a foreign language; travel anxiety survey n=1,526 (Jun 2025), destination survey n=2,551 (May 2026) — [Preply holiday language survey](https://preply.com/en/blog/holiday-language); summarised for hotels — [Hotel-Online, Jul 2026](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/how-language-anxiety-is-influencing-travel-behavior-in-2026-and-what-it-means-for-hotels)
- Global Rescue (Fall 2025, 1,600+ experienced travellers): 59% use translation apps on trips, most often to communicate with locals including ordering food; ~90% find smartphone translation apps useful — [Duck-hub round-up citing Global Rescue](https://www.duck-hub.com/blog/multilingual-menu-statistics)
- American Express Global Travel Trends 2025 (8,000+ adults): about a third of Millennial and Gen Z travellers name translation assistance among the best uses of generative AI in travel; 75%+ likely to seek out a food item that went viral — [same round-up](https://www.duck-hub.com/blog/multilingual-menu-statistics)
- The same round-up states that no government, industry or academic source measures how many restaurants offer multilingual menus in any country, and flags common vendor claims ("reduce order mistakes by 17%", "tourists spend 20-30% more") as unsourced — [Duck-hub](https://www.duck-hub.com/blog/multilingual-menu-statistics)
- Google Lens Translate and Dining filters launched May 2019, aimed at travellers; coverage gives no usage numbers — [Engadget 2019](https://www.engadget.com/2019/05/28/google-lens-dining-translate-rollout/)
- Bain-Swiggy (2024): each online customer orders from 6+ restaurants and 3+ cuisines a year — [Startup Story Media](https://startupstorymedia.com/insights-online-food-delivery-market-set-to-reach-rs-2-trillion-by-2030-bain-swiggy-report/)
- Zomato 2024: Delhi booked 1.25 crore table reservations on Zomato; one Delhi user visited 1,377 unique restaurants in the year (an outlier, not an average) — [Indian Food Times on Zomato 2024 year-end report](https://indianfoodtimes.com/zomato-2024-year-end-report-9-crore-biryanis-5-crore-pizzas-125-crore-reservations-more)

### Inferences
- The 17% who retreat to fast food abroad to avoid ordering mistakes is a direct measure of the pain Tabled claims to remove; it is a lower bound because it counts only those who changed venue, not those who ordered badly or asked the waiter.
- "6+ restaurants and 3+ cuisines a year" per online customer implies a meaningful minority of occasions are at a restaurant or cuisine the diner has not used before, but it is a delivery metric and does not give a first-visit share.

### Gaps
- No survey on share of restaurant visits that are first visits (OpenTable tracks "New" guests per restaurant but publishes no aggregate).
- No survey on share of orders that are dishes the diner has never eaten.
- No Google Lens / Google Translate menu-translation volume data.
- Kim and Mattila (UMass) study on language barriers and ordering accuracy is cited by vendors but was not located.

## Key question 7: Counter-evidence - repeat-heavy behaviour and familiar-dish ordering

### Takeaway
About 30% of diners order the same dish every time (US QSR, UK all-restaurant surveys, 2023), 57% rotate among a few go-to items, only 13% arrive undecided; 74% of US diners prefer a physical menu and a human server; Indian delivery is dominated by biryani and North Indian; Bain calls eating out in India "a special event" at 5 times a month; 23% of urban Indian panelists dine out only on special occasions or never. These are the senior's strongest numbers and they are real.

### Cited Findings
- CRI "Menu Mastery" survey (Sep 2023, ~1,100 US QSR customers via Pollfish): 30% order the same thing every time; 57% have a few go-to items at specific restaurants; 13% visit without a plan for what to order; 86% say a limited-time offer could affect their order — [Sixteen:Nine / Invidis on CRI research](https://invidis.com/sixteen-nine/2023/11/03/old-habits-die-hard-at-qsr-ordering-positions-cri-research/)
- UK (2,000 adults, commissioned by Doritos): 32% always choose the same dish they always have when eating out; 49% want to be more adventurous but fall back on favourites — [Yorkshire Post (search snippet; page 403 on fetch)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/read-this/one-in-three-brits-stick-to-the-same-dish-every-time-they-go-for-dinner-5283765)
- 74% of US diners prefer viewing a physical menu and ordering directly from a server (Harris/TouchBistro, Oct 2024) — [TouchBistro 2025 report](https://marketingdev.touchbistro.com/?p=37255)
- Bain-Swiggy (2024): eating out in India is "a special event" at ~5 times a month vs 25 to 30 in the US and China — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/food-delivery-market-to-grow-18-to-rs-2-trn-by-2030-bain-swiggy-report-124070300980_1.html)
- Rakuten (2025) India: 16% dine out only on special occasions, 7% never — [Rakuten Insight PDF](https://insight.rakuten.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/RI-Dining-Out-2025.pdf)
- Zomato 2024: biryani top dish for the 9th year (9.14 crore orders); North Indian the most trending cuisine on Zomato Food Trends — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/latest/trends/story/from-biryani-pizza-to-coca-cola-cans-condoms-this-is-what-india-ordered-from-zomato-blinkit-in-2024-458750-2024-12-27)
- Korean food remains "a niche base of consumers who are experimental and willing to pay a premium" (Euromonitor, 2021) — [Euromonitor](https://www.euromonitor.com/article/the-rise-and-future-of-k-food-in-india-food-provenance-in-consumer-decision-making)
- Japanese restaurants: 528 in all of India, Oct 2025 — [Indian Retailer](https://www.indianretailer.com/franchisetv/archives/article/novelty-network-how-sushi-restaurant-brands-are-scaling-through-franchising)

### Inferences
- The repeat-ordering surveys cut both ways: 30% lock in a dish, but 70% do not, and the "rotate among go-tos" majority still had to learn those go-tos on earlier visits. The surveys are also from QSR and UK pub contexts where menus are simple and in the diner's own language.
- The strongest counter-argument is per-restaurant density, not aggregate volume: at Indian metro frequencies a single casual-dining restaurant may see only a handful of "confused tables" a day (see sanity check below). Tabled's value has to be framed per table-with-a-moment, or bundled with ordering and memory features that fire on every table.

### Gaps
- No Indian survey on same-dish repeat ordering or on share of dine-in occasions at a "usual" restaurant.

## Key question 8: Bottom-up estimate of annual dine-in occasions in Indian metros where at least one diner does not recognise at least one dish

### Takeaway
Base case: roughly 130 million table-occasions a year in the top 8 Indian metros contain the moment (range 45 to 300 million), out of roughly 370 million table-service occasions, i.e. about one table in three. The occasion count is sourced; the "does not recognise a dish" probability is an explicit assumption because no survey measures it.

### Cited Findings (inputs)
- Eating-out consumer base 320 to 340 million nationally, 70% of consumption from top 50 cities and upper-middle/high income (Bain-Swiggy 2024) — [Startup Story Media](https://startupstorymedia.com/insights-online-food-delivery-market-set-to-reach-rs-2-trillion-by-2030-bain-swiggy-report/)
- Metro dine-out frequency 3.09 (Hyderabad) to 3.6 (Chennai) per month, 2023-24 — [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/telangana/hyderabads-food-services-industry-booms-nrais-2024-report-1809982); [Hospitality Lexis](https://hospitalitylexis.media/national-restaurant-association-of-india-nrai-launches-india-food-services-report-2024-chennai-chapter/)
- Indians usually dine with family (72%) or friends (65%), 12% alone; 52% usually use casual dining, 45% fine dining, 50% street food, 50% fast-food chains, 47% food courts (multi-select, 2025) — [Rakuten Insight PDF](https://insight.rakuten.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/RI-Dining-Out-2025.pdf)
- Casual dining 48% of organised food services value FY24; organised 43.86% of market — [Tea Post citing NRAI](https://teapost.in/doc-files/tea-post-limited-industry-report.pdf)
- 30 to 32% order the same dish every time; 13% arrive undecided (US QSR 2023, UK) — [Invidis/CRI](https://invidis.com/sixteen-nine/2023/11/03/old-habits-die-hard-at-qsr-ordering-positions-cri-research/)
- 6+ restaurants and 3+ cuisines per online customer per year (Bain-Swiggy 2024) — [Startup Story Media](https://startupstorymedia.com/insights-online-food-delivery-market-set-to-reach-rs-2-trillion-by-2030-bain-swiggy-report/)
- Hyderabad outlet counts: 74,807 total, 41,144 organised, 16,379 cloud kitchens, 13,544 QSR, 6,468 casual dining — [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/telangana/hyderabads-food-services-industry-booms-nrais-2024-report-1809982)

### Inferences (the estimate, every assumption shown)

Step 1 - Eating-out consumers in the top 8 metros (Delhi NCR, Mumbai MMR, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, Kolkata, Pune, Ahmedabad).
- ASSUMPTION (not sourced in this research): combined population of these 8 urban agglomerations ~115 to 120 million. Not fetched; standard UN-style estimates.
- Sourced anchor: 320 to 340 million eating-out consumers nationally, 70% of consumption in top 50 cities (Bain 2024).
- Assume 60% of metro residents are eating-out consumers → low 50m, base 70m, high 90m.

Step 2 - Dine-out occasions per consumer per year.
- Sourced: 3.09 to 3.6 dine-out visits a month (NRAI 2023-24 city data). Use low 36/yr, base 40/yr, high 44/yr.
- Person-visits per year: low 1.8bn, base 2.8bn, high 4.0bn.

Step 3 - Restrict to table-service occasions (casual dining, fine dining, cafes; exclude QSR, street, food court, canteen, cloud kitchen).
- Sourced hint: casual dining is 48% of organised value; Rakuten shows casual dining 52% and fine dining 45% "usually" alongside street food 50% and QSR 50%.
- ASSUMPTION: 35% (low), 40% (base), 45% (high) of dine-out person-visits are table-service.
- Table-service person-visits: low 0.63bn, base 1.1bn, high 1.8bn.

Step 4 - Convert to table-occasions.
- Sourced: 72% usually with family, 12% alone (Rakuten India 2025). ASSUMPTION: average party 3.0 (low case 3.0, high case 2.8).
- Table-occasions per year: low ~210m, base ~370m, high ~640m.

Step 5 - Share of table-occasions at an "unfamiliar" venue (non-native cuisine, fine dining, first visit to that restaurant, or diner is travelling/migrant).
- No direct source. Indirect: 6+ restaurants and 3+ cuisines per year per online customer; Korean 17x and regional 2 to 8x growth; 45% "usually" use fine dining; 528 Japanese outlets nationally (so East Asian alone is tiny).
- ASSUMPTION: 20% (low), 30% (base), 40% (high).

Step 6 - Probability the moment occurs at the table.
- Per-person probability of meeting at least one dish they do not recognise and are considering: at an unfamiliar venue assume 0.5 (base), 0.35 (low), 0.55 (high); at a familiar venue 0.05 (low), 0.10 (base), 0.15 (high). These are judgement calls; the 70% who do not lock in a dish and the 13% undecided (QSR, simplest menus) are the only quantitative anchors.
- Party of 3: P(at least one) = 1 - (1-p)^3 → unfamiliar venue: low 0.73, base 0.875, high 0.91; familiar venue: low 0.14, base 0.27, high 0.39. To be conservative on "considering" (not just "seeing"), haircut familiar-venue values by half: low 0.07, base 0.14, high 0.20.
- Blended probability per table-occasion: low 0.20x0.73 + 0.80x0.07 = 0.20; base 0.30x0.875 + 0.70x0.14 = 0.36; high 0.40x0.91 + 0.60x0.20 = 0.48.

Step 7 - Result (top 8 metros, per year).
- Low: 210m x 0.20 ≈ 42m table-occasions with the moment.
- Base: 370m x 0.36 ≈ 133m.
- High: 640m x 0.48 ≈ 307m.
- Add rest of India (tier II/III at 32% of market value and growing 2x faster): scale base by ~1.5x → ~200m nationally, but this is weaker because dine-out frequency and non-native cuisine density outside metros are lower.

Sanity checks.
- Supply side: Hyderabad has ~6,500 casual dining + ~4,800 other organised non-cloud, non-QSR outlets. Extrapolating across 8 metros gives roughly 60,000 to 100,000 table-service outlets. Base-case 370m table-occasions / 80,000 outlets ≈ 4,600 tables per outlet per year ≈ 13 tables a day, of which ~4.5 contain the moment. That is low for a real casual-dining restaurant (typical 30 to 100 covers a day), which suggests either the consumer-side frequency is understated or the outlet count includes many tiny places. Either way, per-restaurant density of the moment is "a few tables a day", which is exactly the senior's objection in per-venue terms; the aggregate is large because the venue count is large.
- Demand side cross-check: Zomato alone recorded 1.25 crore (12.5m) reservations in Delhi in 2024; if Delhi NCR is ~25% of metro table-occasions (base ~93m), Zomato reservations would be ~13% of them, consistent with "45% usually reserve, by phone or app" (Rakuten) once walk-ins and phone bookings are netted out.
- Frequency cross-check: Zomato Gold members dined out 2.8 to 3.3 times a month (2019), consistent with NRAI 2023-24 city figures.

What this means for the objection.
- "Too occasional" is true per diner (about 1 in 3 table-service visits, roughly once a month for the average metro consumer) and per restaurant (a few tables a day). It is false in aggregate (order of 100 million table-occasions a year in 8 cities), and the share is rising: non-native cuisines are growing 4 to 17x on delivery, hyper-regional Indian 2 to 8x faster than mainstream, pre-booked tables 7x faster than walk-ins, and 89% of Indians cannot read an English menu fluently.
- The business implication is that Tabled's wedge cannot be priced on the "confused table" alone; the restaurant pays for every table (ordering, upsell, memory, language), and the unfamiliar-dish moment is the demo, not the unit of value.

### Gaps
- Metro population and eating-out penetration by city were not fetched; Step 1 is the least-sourced step.
- Steps 5 and 6 (unfamiliar-venue share and recognition probability) have no direct survey evidence anywhere; a 300-person intercept survey at 10 Bengaluru restaurants would replace both assumptions and is the single highest-value piece of primary research Tabled could run.
- Dine-in share by cuisine, first-visit share, and never-had-it share remain unmeasured for India.
