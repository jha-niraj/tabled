# Competitive landscape: AI dine-in menu assistants and QR table experiences (India and global, as of October 2026)

Scope note: research run 2026-10-09. Web search budget ran out before the last sweep (Google's food-ordering economics, a non-vendor India QR-usage source, me&u's 2025-26 numbers, Zomato Pay 2025 terms, and a final TechCrunch pass for AI-waiter seed rounds), so those are listed as gaps rather than guessed. Every number below is as reported by the linked source; company-published figures are flagged as such.

## Key question 1: India - what Petpooja, DotPe, Thrive, Restroworks, Dineout, EazyDiner, Zomato Dining, Magicpin, UrbanPiper, Torqus and QR-menu startups actually offer for dine-in in 2026

### Takeaway
No Indian incumbent sells a diner-facing AI table host. The POS players (Petpooja, Restroworks) sell QR "scan and order" as a plain ordering add-on with no dish explanation, recommendation or voice layer; the aggregators (Zomato District, Swiggy Dineout) sell discovery, reservations, discounts and pay-via-app, and their AI is either delivery-side (Swiggy Guru) or pre-visit (Zomato AI for Gold). The only Indian products that resemble Tabled are two small, unfunded, self-described vendors (NWaiter at Rs 999/month and DineOpen at $20-99/month) whose "AI virtual waiter" is an order-taker in Indian languages, not a dish explainer with memory or a feedback loop.

### Inferences
- Indian restaurant tech is organised around billing (POS), delivery aggregation and discovery/discounts. The table itself is served by cheap QR ordering add-ons whose job is to cut waiter load, not to help the diner choose. Tabled's wedge (explain, narrow, remember, ask afterwards) is not occupied by anyone with funding.
- The two closest Indian analogues (NWaiter, DineOpen) prove the feature is buildable on Sarvam-class APIs for under Rs 1,000/month, which sets the price ceiling a restaurant will expect and means "voice in Indian languages" alone is not a moat.
- The NRAI history (2019 Zomato Gold logout, 2022 Dineout logout, Zomato Pay commission complaints) shows restaurants distrust aggregators at the table. A restaurant-paid, restaurant-owned host is positioned on the restaurant's side of that fight.

## Key question 2: Global - Sunday, Popmenu, Toast, Owner.com, SevenRooms, OpenTable, Resy, Yelp, TouchBistro, Square, Lunchbox, Bbot, Mr Yum/me&u, Qlub, Allset, Wix and the 2025-26 AI "menu concierge" and "AI waiter" startups

### Takeaway
Globally the money went to three adjacent things: pay-at-table (Sunday, Qlub, me&u, GoTab, Bbot), operator-side AI (Toast IQ, Popmenu, Owner.com, Dishio, YC's 2025-26 batch) and pre-visit AI concierges owned by discovery platforms (OpenTable Concierge, Yelp Assistant, Google Ask Maps). The diner-facing, at-the-table AI host is only being attempted by micro-vendors (IAMenu, ai-waiter.com, QRSur) with no reported funding; the one 2020 "AI waiter" (w8r.ai) is dead. The COVID QR cohort consolidated (Mr Yum + me&u merged then cut staff; Sunday exited 60% of markets; Bbot and SevenRooms were absorbed by DoorDash; Allset by SoundHound).

## Key question 3: Did the COVID-era QR menu wave die back, and why?

### Takeaway
Usage kept rising while liking fell: in the US, QR menu usage went from 42% (2022) to 65% (2025) yet only 37% like it and 58% want paper back; US and UK surveys from 2024 show 81-93% preferring physical menus. Restaurants reverted mostly in full-service and fine dining; QR persisted where it replaces a counter queue or a bill wait. India data is thin and vendor-sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Ipsos Consumer Tracker (July 21, 2025): QR menu usage 42% (2022), 57% (2023), 65% (latest); only 37% like the experience; 58% want to return to paper; 39% hope QR continues; "saves paper" approval fell from 55% to 46% — [Ipsos](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/qr-code-menus-are-growing-even-less-popular)
- Toast blind survey (Sept 2024, n=850): 81% prefer a physical menu, 1% prefer QR — [Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/fn-dish/news/paper-menu-qr-code)
- PYMNTS/Paytronix (2024, ~2,400 US consumers): 31% positive about QR menus; Technomic via WSJ: 88% would rather have paper; NYT (via Business Insider): many restaurants switching back, citing guest dislike, cost and older guests — [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/business/articles/people-hate-qr-code-menus-164525439.html); [Eat Your Books](https://wp.eatyourbooks.com/blog/2024/05/31/have-we-seen-the-last-of-qr-code-menus)
- UK, Lightspeed 2024: 85% prefer physical menus in casual dining, 93% expect them in fine dining; 35% would ask for a paper menu if offered a QR — [Lightspeed UK](https://www.lightspeedhq.com/uk/blog/2024-state-of-the-uk-hospitality-industry-data-that-brings-innovation/)
- UK counter-claim: DineOpen cites a "2025 UK Hospitality Association survey" with 67% preferring QR (81% under-35); no such survey could be found — [DineOpen (vendor, unverified)](https://www.dineopen.com/blog/free-qr-code-menu-uk-restaurants)
- India: POSist PRIME estimated roughly one-third of Indian restaurants used QR ordering during COVID; Restroworks claims 10-20% higher average check after digital menus (no study cited) — [Restroworks blog](https://www.restroworks.com/blog/indian-restaurant-industry-statistics/)
- India vendor claims (unverified, internally inconsistent): DineOpen attributes "78% of Indian customers prefer QR menus" to an NRAI 2025-26 report and gives urban adoption anywhere from 68% to 85% across its own pages — [DineOpen (vendor)](https://www.dineopen.com/blog/qr-code-menus-future-trend-2026)
- Operator economics that kept QR alive: Toast's 10-12% processing lift and 18% more tipped QSR transactions (2022 internal data) — [Toast news](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/toast-enhances-mobile-order-and-pay-solutions-to-help-restaurants-increase-sales-collect-guest-data-and-navigate-labor-challenges)
- Company outcomes from the wave: Sunday exited 60% of markets in 2022 — [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/restaurant-payments-app-sunday-cuts-staff-exits-60-of-its-markets/627312); Mr Yum cut 17% then merged with me&u, which cut 10% more in 2024 — [AFR via Tiger](https://itiger.com/news/2485220485); Lunchbox cut a third — [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/lunchbox-reportedly-lays-off-60-employees/627898/)

### Inferences
- The wave did not die; the "PDF behind a QR" format did. Diners punish QR when it is a worse menu; they tolerate it when it removes waiting (ordering at the counter, paying the bill). A host that makes the phone a better menu than paper (photos, video, questions, language) is attacking the exact reason the 58% want paper back, but it must earn the scan every time because the scan is now a mildly negative default.
- India's pre-COVID habit of ordering through a captain, plus vendor-only data, means no one actually knows India's QR persistence rate. This is a research gap Tabled could close cheaply by counting QR codes on tables in a few hundred restaurants.

### Gaps
- No independent India survey on QR menu usage or sentiment 2024-26 (NRAI report not located; search budget ran out before an Economic Times pass).
- No 2025 survey showing a decline in restaurant-side QR deployment; the evidence is on sentiment, not removal rates.

## Key question 6: Honest read - where a startup can win (restaurant-owned data, verified allergens, Indian-language voice, memory across visits, feedback loop) versus where incumbents have structural advantage

### Takeaway
The space is empty in India and crowded-adjacent globally: nobody funded is doing restaurant-owned, at-table, dish-level AI with memory and a feedback loop, but every neighbour (POS, pay-at-table, aggregator, Google) could bolt on an LLM chat in weeks. The defensible parts are the ones that need the restaurant's cooperation and time (verified dish data and allergens, video, per-diner memory per venue, post-meal feedback tied to the dish), not the parts that are an API call (chat, translation, Indian-language STT via Sarvam, which Swiggy also rents).

### Cited Findings
- Sarvam voice models cover 11 Indian languages and are available to Swiggy as a partner, so vernacular voice is a rentable commodity — [Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/swiggy-introduces-ai-voice-ordering-with-sarvam-ai-across-11-indian-languages/)
- NWaiter already ships vernacular voice ordering at Rs 999/month with zero setup fee — [NWaiter pricing](https://nwaiter.com/pricing); DineOpen bundles "AI voice ordering" in a $20-50/month POS — [DineOpen pricing](https://www.dineopen.com/pricing)

### Inferences
Where a startup can win:
- Restaurant-owned, verified dish data (ingredients, allergens, spice, portion, video) is the one input no incumbent has and none is paid to collect; Google and Yelp build from crowd photos and reviews, aggregators from delivery menus. The hallucinated-price episode shows what crowd-sourced AI does to a menu.
- Per-venue memory across visits requires the restaurant's consent and the diner's identity at the table; aggregators hold the diner identity but restaurants refuse to let them own the relationship (NRAI history). A restaurant-paid host is the only party both sides will let hold that memory.
- The post-meal "was it what you expected" loop closes on the dish, not the restaurant rating. Nobody found does this at the dish level; it is also the data that makes the recommendations better, which is a compounding asset the chat layer alone is not.
- India timing: Petpooja, DineOpen and NWaiter prove restaurants will pay Rs 1,000-5,000/month for table software, and Sarvam makes vernacular voice cheap. The price anchor is low, so the product has to be sold on covers and check size, not on "AI".

Where incumbents have structural advantage:
- Petpooja (100k+ restaurants, fresh capital for AI) and Restroworks (25k) own the POS and the KOT; a host that cannot push an order to the KOT is a brochure. If Petpooja adds a chat layer to Scan & Order it reaches more restaurants in a quarter than a startup can in years. Mitigation is to integrate with Petpooja first, not compete with it.
- Pay-at-table owners (Sunday, Qlub, GoTab, DoorDash Tableside) already hold the table QR and the payment margin in their markets; none is in India today.
- Zomato District and Swiggy Dineout own the diner before arrival and the discount lever; they can bundle a table assistant into Gold/One. Their AI roadmap (Nugget, Guru, Sarvam, MCP) shows capability. What stops them is incentive: a restaurant-branded host does not raise their NOV and would antagonise NRAI.
- Google and Yelp will own the "what is this dish" question before the diner sits down (Ask Maps live in India since March 2026). The startup's answer must be about this kitchen tonight, for this diner, or it loses to a free pre-visit answer.
- The scan itself is a headwind: most diners prefer paper when the phone offers a worse menu. The host has to be worth the scan on the first visit, with no memory yet.

Brutal summary: products that already do "most of what Tabled does" at the table are NWaiter (India, voice ordering in 9 Indian languages, Rs 999/month), ai-waiter.com (allergen Q&A, 90+ languages, $0.01/request) and IAMenu (EU, AI waiter recommendations, EUR 19-69/month). All three are small, unfunded and lack memory and feedback loops. Well-funded players with a clear path to the same thing: Petpooja (POS plus capital), Swiggy (Sarvam voice plus Dineout's 52k restaurants), OpenTable (Concierge plus planned personalisation), Toast and Square (Mobile Order & Pay plus Google ordering tie-ins). None has announced it.
