# The "host that already knows you": personalisation, memory, allergen trust and outcome feedback in dining (as of 9 October 2026)

Scope note: this covers Phase 2 of Tabled (optional diner account, taste profile, pre-visit voice note, personalised opening, one-tap "was it what you expected?" and a restaurant-facing "regret rate"). India first, global second. Dates are labelled. Vendor-published numbers are flagged as such. Where the web search budget ran out before a question was fully closed, that is recorded under Gaps rather than guessed.

Headline for the sceptical seniors: the evidence says diners like being recognised and say they want tailored service (strongly so in India), but (a) nobody has made a standalone consumer "taste memory" product retain users at scale, (b) the memory that works commercially lives inside restaurant CRMs and loyalty programmes, not in diner-owned profiles, (c) the one visible attempt to surface cross-restaurant AI memories to staff (OpenTable, 2025) produced a privacy backlash, and (d) dish-level "did it meet expectations" feedback has precedent in Toast and Tattle but no one publishes a regret metric. The DPDP Act makes an opt-in, purpose-specific, separately-consented profile workable, but allergen and dietary data will be treated as high-risk in practice and the 18-month compliance clock runs to May 2027.

## Key question 1: Do diners want restaurants to remember their preferences, and how many find it creepy?

### Takeaway
Stated demand for recognition and tailored service is high (SevenRooms US 2025: 83% open to joining restaurant programmes, 25% say "welcome back" is the most valued staff interaction; Qualtrics 2024: 82% of Indian consumers prefer companies that tailor to them, highest of 23 countries). But the same Indian respondents are also the most privacy-concerned (82% extremely or very concerned), only 20 to 43% are comfortable with any given data type being used, and a decade of OpenTable surveys put "creepy" at roughly a third of diners when the memory feels like surveillance rather than hospitality.

### Cited Findings
- SevenRooms 2025 US Restaurant Trends Report (consumer survey of 1,000 US adults aged 16+, Censuswide, 12 Dec 2024 to 6 Jan 2025; operator survey of 257 decision-makers): 83% of diners are open to joining restaurant marketing programmes but expect "VIP treatment" beyond discounts; 25% say the most valued staff interaction is a simple "welcome back"; 47% want birthday and anniversary promotions; only 20% cite being asked to share data as a deterrent to booking and 14% cite marketing opt-ins; 74% are comfortable with AI in the booking experience — [SevenRooms press release, April 2025](https://sevenrooms.com/press/2025-US-data-report/)
- SevenRooms Australia 2025 report: 82% of consumers value personal interactions, which can decide whether they return — [SevenRooms AU press release, 2025](https://sevenrooms.com/press/2025-AU-data-report/)
- SevenRooms (vendor claim): personalised automated emails earn 12x more revenue per send than mass emails — [SevenRooms 2025 US trends report page](https://sevenrooms.com/research/2025-US-restaurant-trends/)
- OpenTable survey, March 2017: 35% of diners think it is creepy for restaurants to Google them before arrival (up from 31% in a 2015 survey of 6,000+ diners); 56% want restaurants to know their preferred table or seating area — [OpenTable / PR Newswire, 2017](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/robots-ticketing-and-seating-preferences-opentable-research-reveals-what-diners-do-and-dont-want-from-technology-300459812.html); [OpenTable / PR Newswire, 2015](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/opentable-research-reveals-what-diners-actually-want-from-technology-300114080.html)
- Qualtrics XM Institute Q3 2024 Global Consumer Study (23,730 consumers, 23 countries, published March 2025): 64% globally prefer companies that tailor experiences; India highest at 82%. Privacy concern in India: 48% extremely concerned + 34% very concerned = 82% (global 53%). Trust that companies use personal information responsibly: India 68% completely or mostly (global 33%). Comfort with a company using specific data for personalisation, India: purchase history 50%, website visits 57%, identification details 51%, chatbot interactions 42%, agent interactions 42%, location 23%. Globally the most-accepted data type is purchase history (45%) and the least is financial information (12%). Trust lifts comfort by about 8 percentage points per data type — [Qualtrics XM Institute data snapshot, 2025 (PDF)](https://www.qualtrics.com/m/www.xminstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/XMI_RR-DS_ConsumerPreferencePrivacyPersonalization-2025.pdf)
- Salesforce "State of the AI Connected Customer" (7th edition, 2025, global, same figures on the India page): 73% say companies treat them as an individual (up from 39% in 2023); 71% feel increasingly protective of personal information; 64% believe companies are reckless with customer data; only 49% feel brands use their information in a beneficial way — [Salesforce India research page](https://www.salesforce.com/in/resources/research-reports/state-of-the-connected-customer/)
- Gartner, March 2019 (2,500+ customers): 38% will stop doing business with a company if they find personalisation "creepy"; 57% would unsubscribe — [Gartner press release, 11 March 2019](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2019-03-11-gartner-survey-shows-brands-risk-losing-38-percent-of)
- Gartner, June 2025 (1,464 B2B buyers and consumers, North America, UK, Australia, NZ): 53% reported negative emotions during personalised marketing; personalised marketing made customers 3.2x more likely to regret purchases and 44% less likely to buy again; "active" personalisation where the customer sets the parameters boosts confidence and ROI 2.3x versus passive "next best action" and reduces data-usage concerns — [Gartner via GuruFocus, 3 June 2025](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/2905142/gartner-survey-reveals-personalization-can-triple-the-likelihood-of-customer-regret-at-key-journey-points-it-stock-news)
- Accenture Interactive Personalization Pulse Check 2018 (8,000 consumers): 41% find it creepy to get a text from a brand as they walk past a store; 48% left a website and bought elsewhere due to a poorly curated experience — [Accenture newsroom, 2018](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2018/widening-gap-between-consumer-expectations-and-reality-in-personalization-signals-warning-for-brands-accenture-interactive-research-finds)
- Tillster 2025 Phygital Index (1,500 US QSR and fast-casual consumers): about one third belong to no loyalty programme; one third switched favourite restaurant in the past year — [Food On Demand, 14 April 2025](https://foodondemand.com/04142025/the-2025-phygital-index-report-points-to-kiosk-comfort-and-loyalty-confusion/). A Tillster 2026 blog claims 35% are "very interested in smart menus that remember past customizations" and 29% want menus built around personal preferences, attributed to its 2025 survey; this appears only in the vendor's blog, not in the trade coverage of the report — [Tillster blog, 2026](https://www.tillster.com/blog/restaurant-technology-trends)
- Deloitte "Future of Restaurants" 2023 (750 US consumers, March 2023): 40% prefer ordering directly through a restaurant's own app or website vs 13% via third-party apps; 70% at least somewhat likely to use voice-automated ordering at dine-in; 18 to 38 year olds are 16 points more likely to return to restaurants using automation (58% vs 42%). No personalisation statistics in the release — [Deloitte via PR Newswire, 21 June 2023](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/deloitte-the-future-of-restaurants-continues-to-evolve-fed-by-consumer-demand-for-convenience-and-frictionless-digital-experiences-301856060.html)
- Cornell Hospitality Quarterly, 2025 (Yoon, Peck, Shu; 14,689 TripAdvisor reviews, a hotel field experiment N=82, two lab studies combined N=1,002): Study 4 compared guests who personalised their own room via an app with guests told "the hotel stores and uses their guest information and previous hotel usage history data to personalize the room on their behalf", with identical end results. Self-customisers reported higher loyalty intention (M=6.36 vs 6.03, p=.018, d=.34) while satisfaction did not differ (p=.054). Authors conclude guest-initiated customisation builds loyalty via psychological ownership; silent data-driven personalisation does not get that bonus — [Yoon et al., Cornell Hospitality Quarterly 66(3), 2025 (PDF)](https://rinyoon.com/assets/pdf/yoon-et-al-2025-increasing-hotel-loyalty-through-psychological-ownership.pdf); [Cornell news summary, 5 March 2025](https://business.cornell.edu/news/2025/03/05/enabling-hotel-guests-customize-their-rooms-fosters-customer-loyalty/)
- Hospitality AI study (412 hotel guests, 2025, ResearchGate): AI transparency and AI service quality both raise perceived personalisation, which predicts loyalty; privacy concern moderates — [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396333997_Artificial_Intelligence_and_Service_Personalization_in_Hospitality_Impacts_on_Guest_Loyalty)
- Indian regional sample (2025, Tamil Nadu social media users): 68% prioritise privacy over the benefits of personalisation — [IJSSRR, 2025](https://ijssrr.com/journal/article/download/2822/1996/)
- PwC India data privacy survey: 69% of Indian consumers not aware of their rights relating to personal data — [PwC India (PDF)](https://www.pwc.in/assets/pdfs/aware-prepared-indian-consumers-businesses-navigate-new-era-digital-privacy.pdf)

### Inferences
- The pattern is consistent across sources: diners want to be recognised and helped, and they punish personalisation that feels like surveillance or selling. The Cornell and Gartner 2025 findings both point the same way for Tabled's design: let the diner state the profile themselves ("active" personalisation) rather than inferring it silently. That is a defensible product principle, not just a privacy nicety.
- India is the best market in the Qualtrics data for stated appetite (82%) but it is also the most privacy-anxious; both numbers come from the same respondents, so the opt-in must be visibly in the diner's control.
- The "creepy" share has been stable at roughly 31 to 41% across OpenTable, Accenture and Gartner over a decade. Plan on a third of diners refusing memory features regardless of UX.
- Nothing here measures demand for a cross-restaurant diner-owned profile specifically; every survey asks about "restaurants" or "companies" remembering, which is the CRM model.

### Gaps
- No 2025 or 2026 OpenTable survey on personalisation or creepiness was found; the OpenTable numbers are 2015 to 2017.
- No India-specific dining survey on whether diners want restaurants to remember preferences was found before the search budget ran out (Zomato, Swiggy Dineout, EazyDiner consumer reports were not reached).
- Salesforce's India breakdown was not available beyond the global figures repeated on the India page.
- Deloitte's 2025 restaurant consumer figures were only available through vendor summaries; the "82% of executives increasing AI spend" figure is attributed to a Deloitte Q4 2024 survey of 375 leaders but could not be verified against Deloitte's own page.

## Key question 2: Existing diner profiles and taste-profile products - who has made memory work, and who has failed

### Takeaway
Memory works commercially where the restaurant or chain owns it (OpenTable and Resy CRM guest profiles, SevenRooms, Starbucks, Domino's) and the diner never has to maintain a profile. Diner-owned taste profiles (Beli) have cultural traction with Gen Z but no disclosed revenue or retention; standalone AI discovery apps (DoorDash's Zesty) were folded back into the parent app within four months; and the one attempt to surface AI-generated diner tags to restaurant staff (OpenTable, 2025) became a privacy story.

### Cited Findings
- Resy OS guest data (vendor page, 2025): guest profiles "can include visit history, spend patterns, preferences, notes, tags, and special occasions"; staff can see "dining history and preferences to allergies and anniversaries" at a glance; data comes from POS integration (Toast partnership), private guest surveys, automated pre-visit questionnaires and post-visit feedback requests, and auto-tagging of VIPs and regulars; Resy states the data "will always belong to you" (the restaurant) — [Resy guest data page](https://resy.com/join/guest-data/)
- Resy "Reservationships" tip sheet (Sept 2023): restaurants should get guests to invite their party so the venue can "gather intel on the whole party including dietary restrictions, table preference, and any special celebrations"; servers should mention known allergies when reading specials — [Resy tip sheet (PDF)](https://resy.com/resyos/uploads/reservationships-tip-sheet-9.28.23.pdf)
- Resy "Invite a Guest": companions can add allergy information for the restaurant to keep on file — [Resy blog](https://blog.resy.com/?p=20284)
- OpenTable AI "guest insights" controversy (2025): a Michelin-starred restaurant host posted AI-generated tags on diner profiles (drink patterns, spending levels, review habits, last-minute cancellations, "dines longer than the average OpenTable guest"). Tags pull from reservation and POS data across the network; a diner does not need to have booked via OpenTable, only to have an account with matching contact details. OpenTable said the AI "does not process personal guest data" and is used for "high-level classification and categorization of large, anonymized data sets", that insights are "shared with restaurants to personalize service and help prepare their staff for shifts", and that diners can opt out of POS data sharing in settings. The host who flagged it said a single business dinner can mark someone as a high spender — [BroBible, 2025](https://brobible.com/culture/article/opentable-ai-customer-profiling/); [Fox News / CyberGuy, 2025](https://www.foxnews.com/tech/how-restaurant-reservation-platform-opentable-tracks-customer-dining-habits); [OpenTable guest data and security page](https://www.opentable.co.uk/restaurant-solutions/learn/built-to-protect/)
- OpenTable AI Concierge for diners (2025): free conversational restaurant discovery, still live — [OpenTable blog](https://www.opentable.com/blog/concierge-ai-dining-assistant/); [PYMNTS, 2025](https://www.pymnts.com/restaurant-technology/2025/opentable-debuts-ai-powered-concierge-for-diners/)
- DoorDash bought SevenRooms for $1.2bn in cash (announced 6 May 2025, closed mid-June 2025). SevenRooms profiles store "diner preferences and amount spent" to customise future visits; DoorDash framed the deal as giving merchants "direct relationships with customers" and "best-in-class CRM" — [CNBC, 6 May 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/05/06/doordash-announces-1point2-billion-sevenrooms-deal-misses-revenue-expectations.html); [Restaurant Dive, May 2025](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/DoorDash-acquires-sevenrooms-1-billion/747226/); [Wikipedia: SevenRooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SevenRooms)
- DoorDash Zesty: AI social restaurant discovery app launched December 2025 in New York and the Bay Area; shutdown announced April 2026, app dark 17 April 2026; personalised recommendations and conversational search folded into the main DoorDash app, the social feed dropped. DoorDash said it was "testing Zesty's product market fit". No usage numbers disclosed — [Nation's Restaurant News, April 2026](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/doordash-winds-down-restaurant-discovery-app-zesty); [Restaurant Technology News, April 2026](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/04/doordash-shuts-down-zesty-and-brings-restaurant-ai-discovery-into-core-app/); [TechCrunch launch coverage, 16 Dec 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/16/doordash-rolls-out-zesty-an-ai-social-app-for-discovering-new-restaurants)
- Beli (NYC, founded 2021 by Judy Thelen and Eliot Frost): builds a "taste profile" from pairwise this-or-that rankings rather than star ratings; team of five per a WSJ profile; Series A of about $5.3m from Goodwater Capital, 14 Nov 2023; aggregators put total raised at $5.3m to $6.2m; no user counts or revenue disclosed. App Store reviews complain that "profile match percentages are also extremely inaccurate" and that "most of my friends have the app, but few actually use it" — [Wikipedia: Beli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beli_(app)); [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/beli/__dGog-htkIGO9dxx0slXqdOmKWXD_C9Gm8V6ij9YUamA); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/beli); [WSJ via To Vima](https://www.tovima.com/wsj/the-app-that-makes-rating-restaurants-fun-again-and-gets-better-the-more-you-use-it/); [Taste Cooking on Beli and Gen Z](https://tastecooking.com/beli-invites-the-loneliest-generation-to-dine-out/)
- Earlier taste-profile apps: "Taste" (Troy, NY, 2019) turned user data into personalised restaurant recommendations; The Infatuation's "Text Rex" SMS concierge ran about 300 conversations a day in beta; Ness Computing (personalised restaurant recommendations) was acquired by OpenTable in 2014. None is a going concern as a standalone profile product today — [PYMNTS, 2019](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/restaurant-app-taste-data-social/); [Built In NYC on Text Rex](https://www.builtinnyc.com/articles/infatuation-text-rext); [Wikipedia: Ness Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ness_Computing)
- Starbucks Deep Brew: widely cited claims (15% sales lift, 12% higher transaction value, 30% ROI, 3x spend by Rewards members) come from consultancy and marketing blogs, not Starbucks disclosures; the only operating-level data point is 35.5m 90-day active US Rewards members in Q1 FY26 — [The AI Report](https://www.theaireport.ai/articles/how-starbucks-uses-ai-to-make-a-30-roi); [QRStuff citing Starbucks Q1 FY26](https://www.qrstuff.com/feeds/blog/top-qr-code-loyalty-programs-saas-cafes-restaurants-2024)
- Domino's Rewards: relaunched Sept 2023 with a lower earning threshold and "Emergency Pizza"; added 3m members in 2023 (2m after the relaunch) and grew 2.5m to 35.7m in 2024 per CEO Russell Weiner; member deals are selected on order history — [Marketing Dive, 2024](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/dominos-loyalty-relaunch-emergency-pizza-draws-2-million-new-members/708531/); [Customer Experience Dive, 2025](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/dominos-growth-loyalty-website-app-upgrades/740784/)
- India: Zomato folded dining-out into the District app (Nov 2024); District plus Insider clocked Rs 2,495 crore GOV, about 3x Swiggy Dineout; Dineout turned profitable in Q4 FY25. Neither product documents dish-level ratings or a diner taste profile; Dineout offers restaurant-level filters by rating, cuisine and discount. In May 2025 both Zomato Gold and Swiggy One members lost their rain-surcharge exemption, with reported member backlash that "the benefits no longer justify the cost" — [The CapTable, April 2025](https://the-captable.com/2025/04/swiggy-dineout-scenes-zomato-district-catchup/); [Business Standard, 16 May 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/zomato-swiggy-rain-surcharge-premium-members-profitability-efforts-125051601134_1.html)
- Zomato dropped detailed food, service and ambience sub-ratings years ago in favour of a single overall score "for simplicity", then (May 2020) split restaurant ratings into Dining and Delivery. No evidence of dish-level ratings on Zomato dining — [Zomato on X, May 2020](https://x.com/zomato/status/1263353057061990402); [Zomato blog on rating changes](https://www.zomato.com/blog/changing-zomatos-rating-system-2)

### Inferences
- The commercial pattern is "restaurant-owned memory, diner-invisible". Resy, OpenTable and SevenRooms all monetise guest profiles by selling to the venue, with data populated from POS and staff notes, not from diners curating a profile. A diner-owned profile that the diner chooses to share with a venue is a different, untested model; the closest analogue (OpenTable's network-wide AI tags) shows what happens when the diner did not knowingly consent.
- Standalone discovery and taste apps have repeatedly either been acquired into reservation platforms (Ness, Urbanspoon) or folded back into a delivery super-app (Zesty). Beli is the exception and survives as a small social ranking game, not as a memory that restaurants consume.
- For Tabled, the honest reading is: the restaurant-facing value of "mild, no peanuts" shown to a waiter is proven (Resy tells venues to collect exactly this); the diner-facing value of maintaining a cross-venue profile is unproven; and the opt-in sharing step is where OpenTable got burned.
- Loyalty programmes with memory (Domino's, Starbucks) grow when the incentive is concrete and immediate (free pizza, stars), not because of the memory itself.

### Gaps
- No retention, DAU or churn figures exist publicly for Beli, Zesty, OpenTable AI Concierge, or any taste-profile app; failure is inferred from shutdowns and acquisitions, not from disclosed metrics.
- "Rex", "Tastebud", "Pear" and "Haven" returned no relevant dining-profile startups; the only "Rex" found is a tiny App Store listing with one rating. The search budget ran out before Crunchbase, Inc42 and Product Hunt could be checked for 2024 to 2026 Indian taste-profile startups.
- Zomato Gold and Swiggy One paid-member counts could not be verified from company filings (a restaurant-advice site claims 3m+ and 2.5m+ but with unclear sourcing).

## Key question 3: Voice as input in India

### Takeaway
Google's most authoritative statement is that about a third of search queries in India are spoken versus 3 to 4% in the US (Prabhakar Raghavan, June 2023); WhatsApp reports 7 billion voice messages a day globally and calls voice "the preferred form of communication" in many situations (2022). Vendor-neutral, current, India-specific evidence that users prefer voice over typing in Indian languages is thin; most 2025 to 2026 figures circulating online are marketing blogs citing each other.

### Cited Findings
- Prabhakar Raghavan (Google SVP), reported 22 June 2023: "In India, about a third of our search queries are spoken. That is in contrast to like three or 4% in the US." He attributed it to cultural comfort talking into phones and said Google is focusing on Hinglish alongside Indic languages — [Inc42, 22 June 2023](https://inc42.com/buzz/about-third-search-queries-india-spoken-top-google-exec/)
- Google (Dec 2019): local-language searches rose from 2% of all searches in 2014 to 20% in 2019; voice search in Hindi since 2014 — [Business Standard, 2 Dec 2019](https://www.business-standard.com/article/companies/20-of-online-searches-carried-out-in-local-languages-says-google-119120201385_1.html)
- Google (2026): Search Live expanded to Marathi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Gujarati, Kannada and Malayalam, on top of English and Hindi — [Induji Technologies summary, 2026](https://www.indujitechnologies.com/blog/voice-search-multilingual-india); earlier eight-language voice search launch — [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/google-announces-voice-search-support-eight-indian-languages-66770)
- Google-KPMG "Indian Languages: Defining India's Internet" (April 2017): 234m Indian-language internet users vs 175m English users; the report argued voice recognition would help Indian-language users "who find text-based search in their regional language a challenge" — [KPMG, 2017](https://kpmg.com/ky/en/home/insights_new/2017/04/indian-language-internet-users.html); [Inc42 on 90% of new users being non-English](https://inc42.com/buzz/internet-users-report-google/)
- WhatsApp (2022): "people send 7 billion voice messages daily on average" and "in many situations, voice messages are the preferred form of communication on WhatsApp" — [India.com reporting WhatsApp's announcement](https://www.india.com/technology/whatsapp-sees-7-billion-voice-messages-daily-unveils-new-tools-5311766/)
- India is WhatsApp's largest market at roughly 535.8m users (third-party estimate, 2026) — [Hyperleap, 2026](https://hyperleap.ai/blog/whatsapp-statistics-india-2026)
- Sarvam AI: speech-to-text covering all 22 scheduled languages with code-mixing "out of the box", streaming dictation under 250 ms, and (Sept 2025) voice plus chat agents on WhatsApp in 11 Indian languages via Sarvam Samvaad — [Sarvam speech-to-text](https://www.sarvam.ai/speech-to-text); [Sarvam on X, Sept 2025](https://x.com/SarvamAI/status/1963548479500030279)
- Bhashini (MeitY): translation across 22 scheduled languages plus ASR and TTS available to developers; Jugalbandi WhatsApp bot built on it — [Reboot Democracy on Bhashini](https://rebootdemocracy.ai/blog/voices-in-every-language-bhashadaan-india); [Wikipedia: Bhashini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhashini)
- Unverified marketing-blog figures that should not be cited as fact: "32% of Google searches in India are voice-based, up from 28% in 2025 and 19% in 2022" and "62% of voice queries in India are in Hindi, Hinglish or regional languages" — [Brainguru blog, Sept 2025](https://www.brainguru.in/blog/2025/09/voice-search-optimization-in-india/); [Markhub24](https://www.markhub24.com/post/voice-search-in-india-winning-the-next-wave-of-regional-queries). A secondary source also cites TRAI data that 51% of Indian internet users used voice search or commands, which could not be traced to the primary — [Markhub24](https://www.markhub24.com/post/voice-search-in-india-winning-the-next-wave-of-regional-queries)
- Deloitte 2023 (US): 70% of consumers at least somewhat likely to use voice-automated ordering at dine-in — [Deloitte via PR Newswire, June 2023](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/deloitte-the-future-of-restaurants-continues-to-evolve-fed-by-consumer-demand-for-convenience-and-frictionless-digital-experiences-301856060.html)

### Inferences
- The "one third of Indian search is spoken" figure is three years old but remains the only primary-source number of its kind; it supports a pre-visit voice note as a natural input in India far more than it would in the US or UK.
- The infrastructure (Sarvam, Bhashini, Google Search Live in 9 Indian languages) is now mature enough that code-mixed Hinglish voice is a solved input problem; the open question is social, not technical: will a diner dictate "I am heading to X, feeling light, with two friends" into a restaurant app rather than into WhatsApp to their friends.
- No source was found showing Indian users prefer voice specifically for restaurant or menu tasks.

### Gaps
- Meta has published no India-specific voice-note volume; the "India is 25 to 30% of global WhatsApp message volume" claim is unverified.
- Google for India 2024 and 2025 announcements on Gemini Live or voice usage in Hindi could not be fetched (search budget exhausted).
- No independent survey of Indian users' preference for voice vs typing in non-English languages was found.

## Key question 4: Allergen and dietary rules (India, UK, EU, US) and incident data

### Takeaway
India's FSSAI menu-labelling rule requires allergen declarations only for central-licence and 10+ outlet operators, so most standalone Indian restaurants have no legal allergen duty; the EU makes allergen disclosure for restaurant food mandatory (Reg. 1169/2011 Art. 44, 14 allergens); the UK moved in March 2025 to best-practice guidance that written allergen information should always be available alongside a staff conversation; the US FDA 2022 Food Code (model, state-adopted) asks for written notification of the nine major allergens in unpackaged food. UK data show 38% of allergic diners feel awkward asking staff and only 50% think staff are knowledgeable, which is the gap a trusted written record fills, and UK prosecutions (Rasika Newcastle 2025, Rusty Gun 2025, Zaman manslaughter 2016) show liability lands on the venue when the stated allergy is mishandled.

### Cited Findings
India
- FSSAI Guidance Note on Menu Labelling (23 Feb 2022): menu labelling applies to food service establishments "Having Central License", "Outlets at ten or more locations", and e-commerce FBOs selling their food. Explicitly exempt: "Restaurants/Clubs/Canteens with State Licenses having outlets at less than 10 locations". Required: "The calorific value of the food product/dish, allergen information and logo of vegetarian/non-vegetarian food is to be displayed on Menu Cards/ Menu Boards/Display Boards/Booklets". Allergens to declare: cereals containing gluten, crustaceans, milk, eggs, fish, groundnut and tree nuts, soybeans, sulphite at 10 mg/kg or more; "may be declared in the form of easy to understand symbols or as text". A 25% deviation in calorie values is tolerated. Example given: "Palak Paneer ... Contains Milk & Milk Products, Contains Tree Nuts" — [FSSAI Guidance Note (PDF)](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Guidance_Note_Labelling_23_02_2022.pdf)
- FSSAI Labelling and Display Regulations 2020 compendium (June 2022) — [FSSAI compendium (PDF)](https://www.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Compendium_Labelling_Display_30_06_2022.pdf)
- FSSAI notice of 12 April 2024 responded to complaints that licensing authorities were misinterpreting the menu-labelling requirements, in some cases leading to licence cancellations — [Legality Simplified](https://www.legalitysimplified.com/fssai-clarifies-on-display-of-information-in-restaurants/)

### Inferences
- In India the law gives Tabled no tailwind and no mandate: most of its likely customers (single-site, state-licensed restaurants) have no allergen-labelling duty, allergy awareness is low, and there is no epidemiology to cite. The pitch to Indian restaurants has to be about service quality and Jain, vegan and spice tolerance (which are mainstream in India) rather than allergen liability.
- The UK data are the strongest evidence that a diner-stated, written allergen record creates value: 38% feel awkward asking, only half trust staff knowledge, written information is trusted more than verbal (87% vs 75%), and the regulator is heading toward making written information mandatory. "Allergies are never inferred" aligns exactly with the FSA model (the diner states it, the business confirms in writing).
- Liability cases hinge on what the diner said and what the kitchen did with it. A logged "no peanuts" instruction with a timestamp is evidence in the restaurant's favour only if the restaurant acted on it; it is evidence against them if they did not. Expect restaurants to be wary of an audit trail they do not control.
- The FSA's acceptance of QR-linked allergen matrices (with a non-digital fallback) is a direct precedent for a QR host surfacing allergen data at the table.

### Gaps
- No Indian data on restaurant allergen incidents, FSSAI enforcement counts, or whether Indian restaurants value verified allergen data.
- Which Indian restaurants actually comply with menu labelling (even among the 10+ outlet set) is undocumented.
- The FSA 2024/25 "101 allergy alerts in 2024, 85 in 2025" figures come from a vendor blog and were not verified against FSA recall records.

## Key question 5: Regret or expectation-match metrics - precedent and value

### Takeaway
Dish-level "was it good?" feedback at the moment of payment already exists (Toast Pay Menu Item Review: thumbs up or down on up to three items after mobile payment, weekly reports to the operator; Tattle: item-level surveys pre-filled from the order, about 10% participation, 94% completion). Expectation-disconfirmation is the standard satisfaction theory in hospitality research and has been applied to menu photos vs reality, but nobody publishes a "regret rate" and no validated single-item "met expectations" dish measure was found. Gartner's 2025 finding that personalisation can triple purchase regret is the closest named use of "regret" as a metric, and it cuts against passive recommendation.

### Cited Findings
- Toast Pay Menu Item Review (support doc, current): after a successful mobile payment via the receipt QR code, "guests have the option to leave feedback, but no obligation"; each item gets a thumbs-up or thumbs-down plus optional text; guests can review up to three items, "prioritizing the more expensive items they ordered"; restaurants get an interactive Menu Item Report in Toast Web and a weekly summary email on Thursdays; Toast does not pass guest contact details because it does not "collect guest consent to be contacted upon receiving reviews"; the feature is on by default when Toast Pay is enabled — [Toast support: Optimize Toast Pay With Menu Item Review](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Toast-Pay-Menu-Item-Review)
- Tattle item-level feedback: post-transaction email via Olo integration, survey pre-filled with the guest's menu items, factors "Food Quality", "Accuracy", "Meal Packaging"; uses include refining prep per item, diagnosing item-specific issues (uneven pizza toppings, soggy fries), testing new products, and allocating marketing budget; vendor claims a 94% completion rate and that brands meeting its objectives have an 84% probability of raising guest satisfaction within 30 days; virtual brands earn about 90% of revenue from one or two items — [Tattle blog: menu item feedback](https://get.tattleapp.com/blog/menu-item-feedback-collection/). Tattle separately reports "on average, a 10% survey participation rate" and 94.5% completion on 50 to 60 question surveys — [Tattle FAQ](https://get.tattleapp.com/resources/faq/); [Tattle feedback collection](https://get.tattleapp.com/features/feedback-collection/)
- Ovation (vendor): SMS 2-question surveys with "up to a 20% take rate" vs about 0.1% for long-form surveys, 97% completion; no dish-level feature documented; 35+ operational categories — [Ovation](https://ovationup.com/); [RestaurantTools review, 2026](https://restauranttools.ai/tools/ovation)
- Resy OS: "Private Guest Surveys capture direct feedback in one easily accessible place"; automated post-visit feedback requests — [Resy guest data page](https://resy.com/join/guest-data/)
- Expectation-disconfirmation theory (Oliver 1980) is the standard model: satisfaction follows from performance exceeding, matching or falling short of expectation; restaurant applications include tipping (high-class vs low-class setting manipulation), menu pictures vs reality (satisfaction, trust and intention depend on disconfirmation magnitude, branding and individual disconfirmation sensitivity), hygiene, and online reviews — [Tipping study, IJHM](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0278431903000744); [Pictures vs reality, IJHM 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0278431921001833); [Expectation-confirmation and online reviews](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338440705_Online_Reviews_of_Restaurants_Expectation-Confirmation_Theory)
- Journal of Foodservice Business Research: asked whether food satisfaction is fulfilment of expectations or evaluation of performance; higher task involvement increased the tendency to fold performance perceptions into disconfirmation judgements — [Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15378020801995549)
- Gartner June 2025: personalised marketing made customers 3.2x more likely to regret purchases; "active" personalisation (customer sets parameters) boosts confidence and ROI 2.3x — [Gartner via GuruFocus, 3 June 2025](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/2905142/gartner-survey-reveals-personalization-can-triple-the-likelihood-of-customer-regret-at-key-journey-points-it-stock-news)
- Zomato removed its detailed food, service and ambience sub-ratings in favour of a single average "for simplicity" and in May 2020 split restaurant ratings into Dining and Delivery; no dish-level rating product on Zomato dining was found — [Zomato on X, 2020](https://x.com/zomato/status/1263353057061990402)

### Inferences
- Precedent exists for the mechanic (one tap per dish at payment) and for the operator-side report (weekly item-level summary). Toast's design choices are instructive: optional, capped at three items, biased to expensive items, thumbs not stars, and no contact data passed back. A "was it what you expected?" tap is a small reframing of this, not a new behaviour.
- Nobody has published a regret or expectation-match rate as a restaurant KPI. That is either white space or a sign that operators have not found it actionable; Tattle's framing (item accuracy, prep, packaging) suggests operators want a diagnosable cause, not a mood score. A regret rate will only be valued if it comes with the "why" (wrong spice level, portion, expectation set by the host).
- Participation will be low: 10 to 20% take rates are the vendor-claimed ceiling for SMS and email surveys; an in-flow tap at payment should do better but there is no public benchmark.
- The Gartner regret finding is a warning for Tabled's own recommendations: a host that steers the order and then measures regret is measuring its own error rate. That is defensible as an honest loop, but it means the metric partly scores the AI, not the kitchen, and the restaurant will notice.

### Gaps
- No academic study validating a single-item "met my expectations" dish measure was found; search on order-regret research could not be run (budget exhausted).
- No independent data on what restaurants do with Toast Menu Item Review data or on its response rate.
- Yelp "popular dishes" and dish-mention analytics were not researched.

## Key question 6: Privacy - India DPDP Act 2023 and the 2025 Rules, consent for dietary data, sharing with a restaurant

### Takeaway
The DPDP Rules were notified on 14 November 2025 with an 18-month phase-in (core obligations including consent notices, retention, erasure and security from about May 2027). The Act has no statutory "sensitive data" tier, so dietary and allergy data get the same consent standard as any personal data (free, specific, informed, unconditional, unambiguous; separate per purpose; withdrawal as easy as giving), but practitioners expect health-adjacent data to be treated as high-risk and a hospitality law firm explicitly says dietary disclosures "can shade into health information when they concern allergies, diabetes, or religious restrictions". Sharing a profile with a restaurant makes Tabled a data fiduciary liable for the restaurant's lapses as processor, or makes the restaurant a separate fiduciary needing its own notice.

### Cited Findings
- DPDP Rules 2025 notified 14 November 2025 (Gazette); PIB describes an 18-month phased compliance period; every data fiduciary must issue a separate, clear consent notice stating the specific purpose — [PIB press note, Nov 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressNoteDetails.aspx?NoteId=156054&ModuleId=3&reg=3&lang=2); [PIB PDF](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)
- Phasing per India Briefing (17 Nov 2025): immediate - Data Protection Board set up and definitions; 12 months - Consent Manager registration and Sections 6(9) and 27(1)(d); 18 months - consent notices, purpose limitation, children's data, retention and erasure workflows, security safeguards. Consent notices must be plain-language, state what is collected and why, offer a dedicated withdrawal link and a contact person. Consent Managers must be Indian-incorporated with INR 20m net worth and keep consent records seven years. Retention: personal data cannot be kept beyond one year of user inactivity unless legally required, with 48 hours' notice before erasure; breach: users informed immediately, Board within 72 hours for the detailed report; penalties up to INR 2.5bn (about US$28m) per breach, graded for MSMEs; verifiable parental consent for under-18s with exemptions for healthcare, safety and education — [India Briefing, 17 Nov 2025](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/dpdp-rules-2025-india-data-protection-law-compliance-40769.html/)
- A healthcare-sector guide dates most substantive duties to 13 May 2027 — [Sigosoft](https://sigosoft.com/blog/dpdp-act-healthcare-apps/); the IT SPDI Rules 2011 continue to govern during the transition — [Legal500 on health data and DPDP](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/food-drugs-healthcare-life-sciences/health-data-&-the-dpdp-act%E2%80%94navigating-india's-healthcare-privacy-frontier)
- No sensitive-data category: the Act "applies uniformly to all 'personal data' ... without carving out a separate 'sensitive' category as the SPDI Rules did"; consent must be "free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous"; secondary use requires separate explicit consent; enforcement authorities "are expected to treat such data as high-risk, particularly where misuse could cause discrimination or stigma" — [Legal500: personal vs sensitive data](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/personal-data-vs-sensitive-personal-data-understanding-the-legal-difference); [AMLEGALS health data guide](https://amlegals.com/health-data-and-the-dpdp-act-a-practical-guide/); [K&K healthtech compliance](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/data-privacy-compliance-healthcare-healthtech/)
- PSA Legal (hospitality): restaurants gather "reservation details and table preferences", "dietary disclosures and, increasingly, biometric or facial-recognition data"; dietary requirements "can shade into health information when they concern allergies, diabetes, or religious restrictions"; "A box ticked by default for marketing emails will not pass muster"; bundled consent fails, each purpose needs separate consent, withdrawal must be as easy as giving; restaurants "share customer numbers and dining preferences with online review platforms", marketing tools and aggregators; "The Data Fiduciary remains liable for the Data Processor's lapses"; data must be erased once the purpose is met; properties "often keep guest records indefinitely", which will not be allowed — [PSA Legal, DPDPA and hospitality](https://psalegal.com/issue-ii-when-the-welcome-desk-becomes-a-data-desk-dpdpa-knocks-on-hospitalitys-door/)
- Practical guidance for restaurants collecting phone numbers for loyalty: a kiosk or tablet notice explaining purpose, or a printed privacy notice at the counter; limit retention — [miniOrange FAQ](https://faq.miniorange.com/knowledgebase/restaurant-customer-data-collection/)
- Consent Managers framework under the Rules — [Legal500 on consent managers](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/building-the-consent-layer-consent-managers-under-the-dpdp-rules-2025)
- UK-side precedent for the sharing problem: OpenTable's defence of network-wide AI tags was that accepting its privacy policy "grant[s] OpenTable certain permissions ... including the right to share their data with restaurants"; critics called the "dining preferences" wording vague — [BroBible, 2025](https://brobible.com/culture/article/opentable-ai-customer-profiling/)

### Inferences
- Tabled's Phase 2 design (optional account, hard constraints entered by the diner, explicit opt-in to share with a named restaurant) maps well onto DPDP's consent model: one notice per purpose ("store my taste profile", "share mild/no peanuts with this restaurant for this visit"), a withdrawal link, and purpose-bound retention. The one-year inactivity erasure rule means dormant profiles must be deleted or re-consented, which conflicts with a "remembers you across years" promise.
- Religious dietary rules (Jain, halal) and allergies will be treated as health or belief-adjacent data in practice even without a statutory tier; log the consent string, never infer them, and never use them for anything but the stated purpose. The DPDP Act's "legitimate uses" do not cover this.
- Sharing with a restaurant has two legal shapes: restaurant as Tabled's processor (Tabled liable for the waiter's misuse) or restaurant as its own fiduciary (restaurant needs its own notice and will not have one). Single-site Indian restaurants are not going to run DPDP programmes; Tabled will have to carry the compliance for them, which is a cost and a selling point.
- Penalties up to INR 2.5bn make a dietary-data breach an existential event for a startup, which argues for minimal storage (hard constraints only, no free-text health notes).

### Gaps
- Nishith Desai, Cyril Amarchand and Khaitan explainers were not surfaced by search; conclusions rest on Legal500, AMLEGALS, K&K, PSA Legal and India Briefing.
- The exact Gazette text of the Rules and the Data Protection Board's guidance were not read directly; the May 2027 date is from secondary sources.
- Whether a diner-held profile shared with a venue counts the venue as processor or fiduciary has no regulatory guidance yet.

## Key question 7: Counter-evidence - personalisation fatigue, account creation at the table, QR aversion

### Takeaway
The strongest counter-evidence is behavioural, not attitudinal: 81 to 88% of US diners say they prefer a paper menu to QR, the top complaint is having to get the phone out at the start of the meal (66%), one third of US QSR diners belong to no loyalty programme, vendors admit optional post-order sign-up "rarely breaks single-digit enrollment", and the Indian QR-ordering leader (Petpooja) explicitly sells "no app, no signup" with phone-OTP only at order confirmation. Memory features that require an account at the table are fighting that current.

### Cited Findings
- Toast survey (850 recent US diners, 2024): 81% prefer a physical menu; 26% say QR menu text is hard to read on their phone; 20% are frustrated at having to use their phone at all; only about 1% actively prefer QR; near 90% paper preference among 55+ — [Toast: How guests really feel about QR code menus](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/qr-code-menu-insights); [u2l.ai summary](https://u2l.ai/blog/qr-codes-for-restaurants)
- Toast Canada Consumer Preferences Survey 2025: 8% prefer QR menus over printed, about 20% no preference — [Toast Canada blog](https://pos.toasttab.com/ca/blog/on-the-line/how-to-integrate-qr-code-ordering-in-your-restaurant-or-cafe)
- Technomic (May 2022, 1,000 US consumers): 88% prefer paper menus; 66% dislike needing to get a phone out at the start of a meal; 50% say QR codes lessen the dining experience; 86% of Gen Z prefer paper — [Restaurant Business Online](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/customers-really-dont-qr-code-menus)
- Axios (March 2023): QR code menus topped the list of COVID-era changes people hate — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2023/03/23/covid-changes-stores-restaurants)
- Tillster 2025: about one third of US QSR and fast-casual diners belong to no loyalty programme; one third switched favourite restaurant in the past year — [Food On Demand, April 2025](https://foodondemand.com/04142025/the-2025-phygital-index-report-points-to-kiosk-comfort-and-loyalty-confusion/)
- National Restaurant Association 2024 Technology Landscape (via QRStuff): 52% of US adults participate in a restaurant or coffee loyalty programme; Gen Z: only 9% prefer smartphone app access to loyalty programmes (QSR Magazine via QRStuff) — [QRStuff, 2024 to 2026](https://www.qrstuff.com/feeds/blog/top-qr-code-loyalty-programs-saas-cafes-restaurants-2024)
- Loyalty vendor (unPLUG, no sample given): "brands with optional post-order signup rarely break single-digit enrollment on digital orders"; Bluestone Lane case: 20,715 new members in 90 days after unifying sign-up across channels — [unPLUG Dining](https://www.unplugdining.com/blog/12-best-restaurant-loyalty-program-ideas-that-actually-drive-repeat-orders)
- Petpooja Scan & Order (India): menu opens in the browser with "no app, no signup, and no friction"; the only identity step is a phone-number OTP to validate the order before the KOT fires — [Petpooja Scan & Order](https://www.petpooja.com/poss/scan-and-order)
- Gartner 2025: 53% of customers report negative emotions during personalised marketing; customers exposed to high information volumes were 2.8x more likely to feel time pressure — [Gartner via GuruFocus, June 2025](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/2905142/gartner-survey-reveals-personalization-can-triple-the-likelihood-of-customer-regret-at-key-journey-points-it-stock-news)
- Beli user reviews: recommendations keep suggesting places already visited; match percentages inaccurate; friends have the app but few use it — [Beli App Store reviews via mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/beli/1478375386)
- Zesty folded into DoorDash within four months of launch (Dec 2025 to Apr 2026) — [NRN, April 2026](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/doordash-winds-down-restaurant-discovery-app-zesty)
- SevenRooms counter-signal: only 20% of US diners say being asked for data deters booking — [SevenRooms, April 2025](https://sevenrooms.com/press/2025-US-data-report/)

### Inferences
- The QR-aversion numbers are US and 2022 to 2024; India has no equivalent survey, and Indian QR ordering (Petpooja, DotPe) is widespread at the casual end, so the aversion may be weaker in India. But the Indian vendor that won did so by removing sign-up entirely, which is the opposite of a profile.
- Account creation should happen off the table (pre-visit voice note on WhatsApp, or post-meal after a good experience) and never block the Phase 1 host. The "was it what you expected?" tap must work for anonymous diners or it will never reach the 10 to 20% participation that SMS surveys get.
- Loyalty enrollment grows on concrete incentives (Domino's free pizza) and declines on friction; a memory feature with no immediate payoff is at a structural disadvantage against both.
- The data-sharing deterrent is small in SevenRooms' booking context (20%), which suggests the pre-visit booking moment, not the table, is where diners tolerate giving information.

### Gaps
- No India-specific diner survey on QR menus, logins at the table, or app fatigue was found; Petpooja and DotPe publish no enrollment data.
- Square's Future of Restaurants 2025 consumer data could not be fetched (search budget exhausted).
- No public enrollment or retention benchmarks for in-restaurant web accounts (as opposed to native loyalty apps) exist.
